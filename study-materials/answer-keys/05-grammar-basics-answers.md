# Answer Key — Grammar Basics

**1. B** — "Each" is singular → `is`.

**2. A** — Singular subject "Marie Curie" → `was`.

**3. B** — Possessive `its` (no apostrophe). The committee owns its decision.

**4. B** — Restrictive clause (essential) → `that`, no comma.

**5. C** — "Walking down the hallway, the painting" is a dangling modifier. Use a clear subject: "As I walked..."

**6. B** — Joining two noun phrases ("vivid characters" + "intricate plot") → simple `and`.

**7. A** — Non-essential clause should be set off by commas on both sides.

**8. A** — After an introductory dependent clause ("Although..."), use a comma.

**9. C** — A complete sentence followed by a list → colon.

**10. B** — Contrast between training and not winning → `, but`.

**11. B** — "Neither/nor": verb agrees with the nearer subject ("employees" — plural) → `were`.

**12. C** — Subjunctive after "recommend that" → base form `submit`.

**13. C** — Most concise; cuts redundancy ("In light of the fact that" → "Since").

**14. C** — Contrast: trained hard, but failed → `Nevertheless`.

**15. C** — Non-essential clause; commas on both sides.
