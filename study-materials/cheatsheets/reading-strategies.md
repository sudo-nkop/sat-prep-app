# SAT Reading Strategies Cheatsheet

The Digital SAT Reading section uses **short passages (25–150 words)** with **one question each**.

## Question types you'll see

1. **Words in context** — "As used in the passage, the word X most nearly means..."
2. **Main idea / central claim** — what's the passage primarily about
3. **Purpose** — why did the author write this/include this
4. **Detail / evidence** — what does the passage state about X
5. **Inference / "most logically completes the text"** — fill the blank with what follows
6. **Two-text comparison** — how would author of Passage 2 respond to Passage 1
7. **Quantitative** — passage + chart/table; pick the choice supported by both
8. **Notes-based "rhetorical synthesis"** — student notes; pick the sentence that achieves a stated goal

## Universal strategy

1. **Read the question first.** It tells you what to hunt for.
2. **Read the passage actively** — underline / note the main claim.
3. **Predict** an answer in your own words *before* looking at choices.
4. **Eliminate** wrong answers; the SAT's wrong answers are usually wrong for **specific reasons** (see below).

## Common wrong-answer traps

- **Out of scope** — true-sounding but not in the passage
- **Half-right** — first half matches passage, second half twists it
- **Extreme language** — "always," "never," "completely" when passage was tentative
- **Opposite** — uses the right concept but reverses direction
- **Right answer to the wrong question** — accurate but not what was asked
- **Too narrow / too broad** for main-idea questions

If two choices look identical, find the **one word** that differs — that's the trap.

## Words-in-context strategy

1. Cover the answer choices.
2. Read the sentence and **substitute your own simple word**.
3. Pick the choice closest to your word.
4. Always plug the answer back in to confirm it fits the *connotation*, not just the dictionary meaning.

## "Most logically completes the text" strategy

1. Identify the **direction** of the last sentence (continuing? contrasting? concluding?).
2. Identify the **subject** the text is leading toward.
3. The correct answer mirrors both direction and subject — no new ideas, no extras.

## Two-text questions

- **Map each text first**: what does Text 1 claim? Text 2?
- The right answer reflects what Text 2 *actually says*, applied to Text 1's specific claim.
- Watch for traps that use Text 2's *topic* but not Text 2's *position*.

## Rhetorical synthesis (notes-based)

1. **Read the goal first** — e.g., "emphasize a similarity between the two studies."
2. The right answer hits the goal *exactly* using info from the notes.
3. Wrong answers are usually true (from the notes) but **don't fulfill the stated goal**.

## Pacing

- ~64 minutes / 54 questions ≈ 70 seconds per question.
- Don't camp on hard questions — flag and move on.
- The Digital SAT lets you mark questions and return within a module.

## Reading habits to build *now*

- Read **dense nonfiction**: The Atlantic, NYT op-eds, Scientific American.
- Read **19th-century fiction**: a Jane Austen, Dickens, or Frederick Douglass passage forces you to slow down.
- When you read, ask: "What is the author's *point*? What evidence supports it?"
