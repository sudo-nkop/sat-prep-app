# SAT Grammar & Writing Cheatsheet

The Writing portion tests a **finite** set of rules. Master these and you'll get ~all of them.

## 1. Subject–verb agreement
- Singular subject → singular verb. Plural → plural.
- **Watch for prepositional phrases** between subject and verb:
  - "The *box* of nails *is* heavy." (subject = box, not nails)
- Compound subjects with **and** → plural.
- **Either/or, neither/nor** → verb agrees with the *nearer* subject.

## 2. Pronoun agreement & case
- Singular antecedent → singular pronoun (it, his/her).
- Plural → plural (they, their).
- "Each, every, anyone, someone, neither" are **singular**.
- **Subject pronouns**: I, he, she, we, they
- **Object pronouns**: me, him, her, us, them
- "Between you and **me**" (not I).

## 3. Verb tense & form
- Keep tense **consistent** in a sentence/passage unless time changes.
- "Have/has + past participle" = present perfect (action started in past, relevant now).
- "Had + past participle" = past perfect (one past action before another).

## 4. Punctuation — the big four

### Comma
Use a comma:
- Before a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) joining two independent clauses.
- After an introductory phrase: "After dinner, we left."
- Around non-essential info: "My brother, who lives in Ohio, called."
- Between items in a list of 3+.

**Don't** use a comma to join two independent clauses without a conjunction (= comma splice).

### Semicolon
- Joins two **independent** clauses: "I came; I saw; I conquered."
- Functionally identical to a period for SAT purposes.

### Colon
- After a **complete sentence**, before a list, explanation, or quote.
- "She had one goal: winning."
- The part **before** the colon must stand alone as a sentence.

### Dash (em dash —)
- Pair of dashes = pair of commas (set off non-essential info).
- A single dash works like a colon for emphasis.
- **Never mix** a dash with a comma or parenthesis as the partner.

## 5. Apostrophes
- **It's** = it is.  **Its** = belonging to it. (No apostrophe for possessive "its"!)
- **They're / their / there**, **you're / your**, **who's / whose** — know the difference.
- Singular possessive: **dog's**.  Plural possessive: **dogs'**.

## 6. Modifiers
- A **modifier** must sit next to what it modifies.
- "Walking down the street, the trees were beautiful." ❌ (trees aren't walking)
- "Walking down the street, **I** saw beautiful trees." ✓

## 7. Parallel structure
- Items in a list/comparison should share grammatical form.
- "She likes hiking, swimming, and **to bike**." ❌
- "She likes hiking, swimming, and **biking**." ✓

## 8. Transitions (very common on SAT)

| Purpose | Words |
|---|---|
| Add | also, furthermore, moreover, in addition |
| Contrast | however, but, yet, on the other hand, nevertheless |
| Cause/effect | therefore, thus, consequently, as a result |
| Example | for instance, for example, specifically |
| Sequence | first, next, finally, subsequently |
| Concede | granted, admittedly, of course |

**Strategy**: Read the sentences before and after the blank. Decide the *logical relationship*, then pick the matching transition word.

## 9. Sentence boundaries

A complete sentence (independent clause) needs:
- A subject
- A verb
- A complete thought

**Fragments** are missing one of these. Common fragment trap: a sentence starting with "Which," "Although," "Because," etc., that never gets a main clause.

## 10. Concise wording (Rhetorical Synthesis)
- The SAT prefers the **shortest grammatically correct** option.
- Eliminate redundancy: "annual yearly tradition" ❌ → "annual tradition" ✓
- Cut filler: "due to the fact that" → "because"

## Quick decision tree for punctuation questions

1. **Is what's before the punctuation a complete sentence?**
2. **Is what's after the punctuation a complete sentence?**

| Before | After | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Complete | Complete | period, semicolon, OR comma+FANBOYS |
| Complete | List/explanation | colon |
| Complete | Fragment (extra info) | dash or comma |
| Fragment | Complete | comma |
| Fragment | Fragment | comma or nothing |
